First identify the target scene in the original using its box and reading-order

A view is an independently presented composition: a framed or bordered panel, an inset with its own boundary, a panel whose boundary is partly hidden by another panel, or a borderless composition separated from its neighbors by a clear gutter or spatial break. One continuous composition stays one view even when it shows several moments, repeated figures, a composited portrait or silhouette, or changes of scale. A difference in subject, scale, time or perspective alone does not create a separate view. Conversely, a distinct inset or adjacent region remains separate even when it repeats the same characters, colors or setting. The intended scene is the source scene represented by that crop, not an
incidental piece of an overlapping neighbor. Locate the actual drawn panel edges,
all associated lettering, and any artwork that breaks its frame. Then compare
these specific features with the actual crop at all four edges.

First inventory the separate views on the page, then measure their bounds.
A view is an independently presented composition: a framed or bordered panel, an inset with its own boundary, a panel whose boundary is partly hidden by another panel, or a borderless composition separated from its neighbors by a clear gutter or spatial break. One continuous composition stays one view even when it shows several moments, repeated figures, a composited portrait or silhouette, or changes of scale. A difference in subject, scale, time or perspective alone does not create a separate view. Conversely, a distinct inset or adjacent region remains separate even when it repeats the same characters, colors or setting.
A panel can be a new view even when its background and colors visually blend with
a neighboring panel, if a frame, border, gutter or spatial break separates them. A foreground subject breaking a border can hide part
of another panel's edge; trace the remaining border and keep BOTH panels.

scene. Do not let a large borderless foreground figure absorb a smaller framed
background view. Conversely, perspective and different people within one coherent
composition are not separate panels. After preparing the list, scan the full page
again for any view not represented and for any box containing two views.

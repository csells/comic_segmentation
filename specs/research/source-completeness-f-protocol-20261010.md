> Inventory independently presented panel/view compositions, not every depicted subject, camera scale or narrative moment. Look across the whole image for separate reading regions: gutters or panel frames, inset boundaries, a panel boundary partly occluded by another panel, or a clear spatial discontinuity between borderless compositions. Ordinary foreground/background occlusion is not a panel boundary. Keep one continuous composition together, including composited portraits, silhouettes, overlapping figures, changes of scale and multiple depicted moments, unless the artwork establishes a separate reading region. A difference in subject, scale, time or perspective alone is insufficient. Conversely, retain a distinct inset or adjacent region even when it repeats the same characters, colors or setting. A wall, window, balloon or object contour is not itself a panel boundary. If the visual separation is unresolved, mark uncertainty rather than inventing a split or merging regions.

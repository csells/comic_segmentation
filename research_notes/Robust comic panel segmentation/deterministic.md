# Deterministic comic panel discovery, preprocessing, and precise boundaries

Research checked 2026-10-09. This is a literature/source inspection and proposed experiment plan; no new detector execution, API call, or benchmark was performed. Existing project comparisons are configuration-specific historical results: [detector comparison](/Users/csells/code/csells/comic_segmentation/specs/research/detector-comparison-20261008.md), [existing tools](/Users/csells/code/csells/comic_segmentation/specs/research/existing-comic-conversion-solutions.md). Current failure context supplied by coordinator: page 57 has thirteen curved colored regions with narrow pale tips; current V28 remains fallback and broad component expansion changed 56/150 pages. Those observations motivate experiments; they do not establish a universal best method.

## Which deterministic approaches and open-source implementations deserve attention?

### Takeaway

Classical methods remain useful as cheap, diverse proposal generators and high-resolution boundary refiners. The strongest research direction combines semantic scene identity with pixel-level evidence; there is no verified classical package here that solves arbitrary layouts, protruding artwork, missing borders, and reading order perfectly.

### Cited Findings

- **Uniform-color gutters and recursive cuts:** Chan, Leung, and Komura, *Automatic Panel Extraction of Color Comic Images* (PCM 2007), searches uniform-color stripes, recursively partitions a page, and obtains layout/reading structure from the partition tree. It explicitly considers unknown background color rather than assuming white. The publisher/institution abstract was available; no reproducible public implementation or independently checked numerical comparison was found. [Author institution record](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/automatic-panel-extraction-of-color-comic-images/).
- **Gradient cuts with T-junction reasoning:** Tanaka et al., *Layout Analysis of Tree-Structured Scene Frames in Comic Images* (IJCAI 2007), combines line-density gradients, white runs, and T-junction decisions. Filling detected quadrangles suppresses internal drawing edges before layout analysis. It reports about 80% page success on 672 pages from four comics at 650×1000. Single-scene pages were excluded, nested panels were outside the tree assumption, and black gutters remained future work. This is evidence that task-specific preprocessing can help, but the reported metric does not imply complete-content crops on arbitrary comics. [Original conference paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/07/Papers/464.pdf).
- **Background-connected components plus shape recovery:** Pang et al., *A Robust Panel Extraction Method for Manga* (ACM MM 2014), repairs open boundaries, labels connected page-background pixels, partitions remaining panel blocks, then recovers quadrilateral shapes. On 104 Naruto/Slam Dunk pages it reports 87.9% complete-page success and 91.3% panel success; success required Jaccard overlap above 0.90. It identifies flood-fill leakage into unclosed panels, sensitivity of erosion/dilation to iteration count, and failures when large symbols disconnect content. Its four-corner output is not a solution for arbitrary concave ribbon masks. The transferable idea is separating layout masks from shape recovery. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.ying-cao.com/projects/panel_extraction/files/panel_extraction_paper.pdf).
- **Region growing/morphology:** Ho, Burie, and Ogier, *Panel and Speech Balloon Extraction from Comic Books* (DAS 2012), uses region growing and mathematical morphology. This is a relevant historical baseline, not a verified current implementation. Search exposed the paper abstract; full PDF fetches failed, so no performance numbers or detailed reproduction claims are made. [Authors' research group's bibliography](https://ebdtheque.univ-lr.fr/references/), [paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/DAS.2012.66).
- **Outline detection rather than background segmentation:** Stommel, Merhej, and Müller, *Segmentation-Free Detection of Comic Panels* (2012), combines corners, edges, regions, and global evidence to detect outlining rectangles on structured backgrounds. The author-uploaded paper describes false choices between internal text rectangles and outer panels, misses borderless panels, and reports roughly five seconds per page on a Core2 Quad. This contributes complementary boundary evidence, not a modern speed comparison. [Author-uploaded original paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258423562_Segmentation-Free_Detection_of_Comic_Panels).
- **Structured probabilistic inference predates current VLMs:** Li et al., *A Tree Conditional Random Field Model for Panel Detection in Comic Images* (Pattern Recognition 2015), models connected components, edge segments, and line segments with contextual features; output uses convex polygons. This is learned structured inference, not purely deterministic thresholding. Publisher abstract/highlights were readable; full paper and runnable code were not verified. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031320315000308), [author institution publication list](https://www.wict.pku.edu.cn/cpdp/kycg/index.htm).
- **Kumiko:** OpenCV contour-based detection with JSON geometry and LTR/RTL ordering. Its roadmap still names borderless/intertwined panels, and its own documentation rejects a universal-perfect promise. License is **AGPL-3.0-or-later**, verified from the actual license file. Existing local project evidence already found this heuristic insufficient on this anthology; keep as comparator, not a reason to discard all classical methods. [Upstream README](https://github.com/njean42/kumiko/blob/master/README.md), [license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/njean42/kumiko/master/LICENSE).
- **paneldet — newly discovered, worth a bounded offline trial:** `jsneij/paneldet` chooses between recursive gutter cuts, connected components, and a hybrid using page-specific background/noise estimates. Failed splits may trigger rotation search up to ±8°. Author-reported exact-count accuracy is 69.6% on 92 certain-label pages, 50% on 24 held-out pages, using three books; labels/scorer are private. Thus these are weak author-reported reproducibility evidence, not our results. API coordinates refer to its possibly rotated output image, an integration trap. Actual **MIT** license verified. The repository is very small, with one visible commit; pin source and inspect before running. [README](https://github.com/jsneij/paneldet/blob/main/README.md), [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jsneij/paneldet/main/paneldet.py), [license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jsneij/paneldet/main/LICENSE).
- **PST / ComicPanelSegmentation:** `reidenong/ComicPanelSegmentation` implements Canny edges, probabilistic Hough lines, line extrapolation/merging, and box extraction. It explicitly targets clean digitally delineated panels and admits difficulty with hand-drawn/unclear boundaries. **MIT** verified. Useful for regular born-digital grids and independent line evidence; unlikely to be the primary answer to curved spiral panels. [README](https://github.com/reidenong/ComicPanelSegmentation), [license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/reidenong/ComicPanelSegmentation/main/LICENSE).
- **comics-panel:** `pakkio/comics-panel` describes contour, marker/watershed, and projection-grid fallbacks with white/black/dual/Canny masks and intermediate debug output. README declares MIT, but LICENSE fetch failed; treat license as unverified until the actual checkout is inspected. No quantitative evaluation was verified. Its default maximum region area of 0.5 and row sorting illustrate why default settings cannot establish splash/narrative correctness. [README](https://github.com/pakkio/comics-panel/blob/main/README.md).

### Inferences

- Use three independent kinds of evidence: gutter/background connectivity, outline/line geometry, and semantic panel proposals. Agreement can reduce review work, but correlated agreement is not a calibrated proof of correctness.
- Recursive XY cuts are a good inexpensive regular-grid path. A spiral arrangement is not necessarily representable by full-width recursive cuts; do not force it into that model.
- Connected components can follow curved colored shapes, but a component is not intrinsically one narrative panel. White interiors can fragment a panel; balloons/characters can connect separate panels. A universal `component bounding box → panel` rule is structurally inadequate.
- Detect frame masks and reading viewports separately. A frame border describes composition; the readable viewport must also include assigned balloons, protruding characters, captions, and effects. Non-overlapping boxes are not a valid universal objective.
- Convex hulls/quadrilaterals retain coarse extent but absorb nearby art around concave curves. Exact polygons/masks could improve attribution and verification even when the reader ultimately uses enclosing rectangles.
- Prioritize `paneldet` as a new inexpensive comparator, not as a likely complete replacement. PST is a lower-priority targeted baseline. Recreating older research implementations from papers is less attractive than borrowing the generic ideas unless a reproducible baseline is needed.

### Gaps

- None of the checked sources establishes perfect arbitrary-comic segmentation, exact reading order, or reliable self-certification.
- Comparable wall-clock/memory numbers on our candidate VM do not exist for the new repos. Claims such as milliseconds or superior to neural models are not portable measurements.
- No runnable open release of the 2007/2014/2015 academic algorithms was verified. The 2012 region-growing paper could not be fetched fully; the tree-CRF publisher only exposed preview material.
- `paneldet` private benchmark makes independent replication impossible without our own labeled corpus. Its small/new repository needs source pinning and checks; its reported versions/tags are not a substitute for a pinned commit.

## What image preprocessing can improve discovery without damaging the comic?

### Takeaway

The highest-priority preprocessing is recovering the best existing source pixels, preserving coordinate transforms, and computing several analysis-only views. Global destructive cleanup or generative enhancement is a poor default when the failure is loss of a faint one-pixel artwork tip.

### Cited Findings

- **Native PDF image extraction and selective rendering:** PyMuPDF exposes embedded-image extraction, image metadata/transforms, configurable DPI rendering, and high-resolution clipped rendering. PDF images may carry masks; simply extracting one image does not necessarily reproduce the displayed page composition. These mechanisms support auditing whether current 150-DPI JPEGs discard useful source detail, and rendering only uncertain boundary neighborhoods when necessary. [Official image recipes](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/recipes-images.html).
- **Analysis cleanup can be separate from output:** OCRmyPDF explicitly distinguishes cleanup before recognition from cleanup inserted into final output. Its docs warn that background removal/cleanup can remove desired content and that image processing may rasterize PDFs and reduce quality. This is an applicable preservation pattern, not evidence that OCR cleanup is effective for comic segmentation. [Official cookbook](https://ocrmypdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/cookbook.html).
- **Adaptive thresholding:** OpenCV documents local mean/Gaussian thresholds for varying illumination. This provides a concrete alternative to one fixed near-white threshold for yellowed or uneven scans. [OpenCV thresholding](https://docs.opencv.org/4.12.0/d7/d4d/tutorial_py_thresholding.html).
- **Niblack/Sauvola:** scikit-image supplies local-statistics binarization, especially intended for document images. Keep these as candidate masks rather than assuming a text-binarization technique preserves pale comic colors. [Official example](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/auto_examples/segmentation/plot_niblack_sauvola.html).
- **Local contrast:** OpenCV CLAHE computes local histogram equalization with a contrast limit. It is an available proposal-view transform; the docs do not establish better comic panel detection. [Official histogram tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d5/daf/tutorial_py_histogram_equalization.html).
- **Noise versus edges:** OpenCV distinguishes smoothing methods and documents bilateral filtering for noise reduction while retaining edges. This is a plausible scan-noise control, not proof that it preserves the thinnest pale taper in our source. [Official smoothing tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d4/d13/tutorial_py_filtering.html).
- **Morphology has geometrical consequences:** Erosion removes boundary pixels, dilation adds them, opening removes small foreground structures, and closing fills small holes/gaps. Kernel size must therefore be treated as a geometry-changing decision, not harmless cosmetic cleanup. [Official morphology tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/d61/tutorial_py_morphological_ops.html).

### Inferences

Recommended staged image preparation, all experimental until measured:

1. **Audit actual source resolution first.** Inspect embedded raster width/height, placement transform, masks, page rotation/crop box, and any vector/text overlays. Compare unchanged current renders against native extraction or a lossless high-resolution composite for representative tiny-boundary failures. Upsampling a low-resolution source does not recover missing evidence. Preserve all old source hashes/cache identities and create explicitly versioned alternative analysis inputs.
2. **Keep original readable page and analysis derivatives separate.** Original CBZ/CBR/folder images should remain unmodified in output. For PDF, preserve PDF separately and retain a documented authoritative page render. Geometry lives in authoritative source coordinates. Any resize/rotation/deskew must retain an invertible mapping; transformed region corners must map back before outward pixel rounding. Save crops from the authoritative page, never from thresholded/denoised masks.
3. **Use multi-scale views with different jobs.** Small full-page view for inventory/order; medium color/gray masks for proposals; native pixels around uncertain edges for geometry. Do not blur the sole boundary-evidence image. Adaptive ROI work can lower total pixels and memory compared with full-page high-resolution inference on every page.
4. **Estimate paper/gutter appearance robustly.** Build alternative masks from luminance, chroma, and distance from candidate background colors. Use border samples as evidence, not a rule: full-bleed art can occupy borders, and black flashback gutters invert the normal assumption. Local background models may be preferable on yellowed/shaded scans.
5. **Conditional deskew.** Try only when multiple long line/gutter orientations support a common small scan rotation. A dramatic diagonal panel is not a tilted scan. Compare the unrotated proposal and retain original output; do not force all pages upright from text orientation alone.
6. **Controlled denoise/threshold ensemble.** Compare raw, light bilateral, local-threshold, and optional CLAHE proposal masks on development pages. Do not apply the entire filter chain to every image. A pale narrow colored ribbon can be erased by grayscale thresholding or opening. Keep color evidence and raw pixels as independent channels.
7. **Avoid hallucinated evidence.** No generative restoration, inpainting gutters, AI super-resolution, or whitening in the authoritative image. Algorithmic closing may be useful on an analysis mask, but must not become proof that an original boundary existed.
8. **Preserve spreads until classified.** Do not blindly split at the physical center: a two-page composition can contain panels or balloons crossing the binding. Output mapping must explicitly handle any optional split.

### Gaps

- The actual embedded raster resolution/colorspace/masks of this PDF have not been inspected in this research subtask. It is unproven that a 300-DPI rerender will add genuine information.
- No preprocessing ablation has established benefit on the current book or held-out comics. General document preprocessing evidence is not comic-specific evidence.
- Pixel-exact preservation cannot be promised for arbitrary PDF image extraction without matching the rendered page composition, rotation, clipping, and overlays.

## How should we reconstruct curved boundaries, evaluate them, and rank the next offline experiments?

### Takeaway

Test semantic-seeded segmentation at native resolution and stable pixel-coordinate geometry before spending more on repeated whole-page box prediction. For page 57, a conservative colored-region boundary proposal plus explicit uncertainty can be more useful than another global morphology pass, but it must be tested beyond that page.

### Cited Findings

- **Marker-controlled watershed:** OpenCV describes flooding gradient/intensity topography and using known foreground/background markers to constrain segmentation. Uncontrolled watershed can oversegment from noise/irregularities. Therefore automatic panel-instance seeds are the important upstream input; running watershed alone is not a panel detector. [Official watershed tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d3/db4/tutorial_py_watershed.html).
- **GrabCut / graph cuts:** OpenCV documents color-mixture foreground/background models and graph min-cut, supporting rectangle or labeled-mask initialization. Critically, everything outside the initial rectangle is definite background in rectangle mode. A box already clipping a tip cannot recover that tip unless the region is expanded or mask initialization leaves it uncertain. Its own example needs additional labels to restore missed hair; automatic semantics must supply those labels for our unattended product. [Official GrabCut tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d8/d83/tutorial_py_grabcut.html).
- **Random-walker segmentation:** scikit-image provides a seeded graph-based segmentation implementation and example. This makes it an accessible offline comparator for propagating automatically supplied labels over low-contrast image evidence. No comic-specific result or suitability for the current VM was established. [Official example](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/auto_examples/segmentation/plot_random_walker_segmentation.html).

### Inferences

Proposed representation and process:

- Retain semantic panel ID, order, rough extent, optional interior seed(s), frame mask, required-art mask, boundary-uncertainty band, and final reading viewport. The mask represents visible pixel evidence; it need not hallucinate the hidden shape behind an inset. Permit shared content ownership when appropriate.
- Expand the analysis ROI beyond a predicted box before refinement. Provide rival-panel seeds and gutter/background seeds so the algorithm has alternatives. A panel can contain white artwork indistinguishable from white paper locally, requiring semantic/context evidence and multi-component grouping.
- For colored isolated regions, derive connected components across a *small bounded family* of color/background thresholds. Associate components with semantic seeds, compare boundary stability, and conservatively enclose plausible tips. Instability becomes escalation evidence. Avoid one hard RGB threshold and avoid requiring the whole panel to be a single component.
- Where components touch, compare seeded watershed/GrabCut/random walker in a local ROI. A multi-label method is attractive because rival panels compete for pixels; independent binary segmentations may claim the same neighboring region. No method should automatically inherit the tight VLM box as a hard exclusion boundary.
- Preserve full-resolution integer crop extents. If normalized coordinates use a 0–1000 grid, one unit on the current 1128×1648 analysis image represents about 1.13×1.65 pixels; re-normalizing and rounding repeatedly can matter for tiny tips. Compute source-pixel extents once, floor minimum/ceil maximum, and serialize at enough precision. A small reading margin can preserve uncertain edge pixels, but a fixed huge margin is not segmentation quality.
- Distinguish segmentation shape from reading viewport. A mask that excludes white interiors may still yield a correct safe enclosing box; masking artwork for presentation introduces new failure risks. Initially use masks as internal evidence and render intact rectangular page crops.

Ranked experiments, with expected information rather than promises:

| Rank | Offline experiment | What it resolves | Acceptance / stop signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Audit PDF native image/composite versus existing render on a small boundary-focused sample; measure source-coordinate round trips | Whether apparent tips are real source detail, rendering/JPEG artifacts, or geometry quantization | Source provenance documented; no source/output mutation; exact coordinate mapping. Stop if native source offers no additional evidence. |
| 2 | Frozen semantic proposals + native-color threshold stability/seeded component association; no new model calls | Whether color connectivity can locate narrow tips without broad unwanted expansion | Compare all changed targets and unchanged negatives. Must restore known clipped artwork without stealing a neighboring scene or changing inventory/order. |
| 3 | Same seeds/ROIs, compare marker watershed versus mask-initialized GrabCut and random walker | Whether competing regions split touching/curved shapes more robustly than isolated components | Record seed failure, leakage, missed thin structures, RAM, seconds. Reject approaches needing page-specific manual seeds at runtime. |
| 4 | Run pinned `paneldet` and PST on a diverse cached subset and an untouched external-book subset | New classical baselines and cheap proposal diversity | Score complete-page inventory/order/content, not count alone. Audit rotated output coordinate mapping. |
| 5 | Small factorial ablation: raw/native versus resize; gray versus color; no denoise versus light denoise; fixed versus adaptive background; with/without conditional deskew | Which preprocessing helps which page types, and whether it actually reduces escalation cost | Freeze choices before held-out books. Report paired wins and regressions rather than cherry-picked attractive examples. |
| 6 | Raster masks → conservative polygons / uncertainty bands, compare with boxes | Whether richer internal geometry improves clipping/ownership checks and reader framing | Reader still shows complete page first, then intact content; polygon complexity is bounded; no automatic permanent masking yet. |

Evaluation design recommendation:

- Maintain separate development, regression, and untouched book-level test partitions. The current anthology and known pages 18/21/40/57/150 are development/regression data, not external validation. Include color western comics, black/white manga, RTL, clean grids, irregular/overlapping/borderless scenes, shared balloons, splashes, aged scans, skew, full bleed, double spreads, and long webtoon pages.
- Measure count/inventory, merges, false splits, order, required-art/text omission, extraneous neighbor intrusion, fallback rate, and uncertainty calibration separately. A near-perfect IoU can hide a tiny clipped face/letter/taper; boundary/content failures need their own metric. Do not optimize only an average mAP or exact count score.
- Use test-only annotations for checking masks/crops, never as runtime geometry or decision input. Record annotation ambiguity: panel frame, narrative scene, and best mobile viewport can differ legitimately.
- Budget is end-to-end dollars per *accepted whole page*, including unsuccessful proposals, judges, repairs, latency, local hardware, and fallbacks. A faster local proposal pays off only if it reduces total accepted-page work at equal quality.
- Abstention should remain explicit. Full-page fallback preserves readable data but must count as unsegmented output, not successful panel discovery. Universal perfection is a design ambition; evidence must state domain coverage and observed error rates.

### Gaps

- These refiners are untested here; seeds and panel ownership are likely harder than the pixel solver. White-to-white boundaries may be visually underdetermined even at native resolution.
- No public benchmark found in this subtask captures every application-specific failure such as a shared balloon retained in every relevant reading viewport.
- Tiny source fringe versus genuine artwork may require a documented annotation tolerance. A verifier that flips accept/reject on unchanged crop bytes cannot serve as the sole acceptance oracle; repeatability must be measured separately.
- The disposable VM has limited RAM. Native-resolution graph methods should be bounded to ROIs and measured before full-page trials; memory estimates or runtime claims cannot replace execution evidence.

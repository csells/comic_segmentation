A view is an independently presented composition: a framed or bordered panel, an inset with its own boundary, a panel whose boundary is partly hidden by another panel, or a borderless composition separated from its neighbors by a clear gutter or spatial break. One continuous composition stays one view even when it shows several moments, repeated figures, a composited portrait or silhouette, or changes of scale. A difference in subject, scale, time or perspective alone does not create a separate view. Conversely, a distinct inset or adjacent region remains separate even when it repeats the same characters, colors or setting. A portrait, figure, silhouette or other image composited inside one framed panel's artwork, without its own frame, border or gutter, belongs to that panel's view even when it shows a different moment, subject or scale; it is not a separate view. A distinct inset with its own frame or border remains a separate view. A wall, window, balloon, foreground object or perspective change inside one continuous view does not itself create a separate narrative view. Do not split a continuous scene merely to make smaller rectangles.

Content that belongs to no single view is not itself a view and does not make a view uncertain: captions, sound effects or balloons that straddle several panels without a tail or other visible connector; loose background elements such as debris, particles or stars in a continuous borderless background shared by several views; and unreadable slivers of other panels cut off at the outer page edge, for example from the facing page of a spread. This never excuses a distinct view, inset or panel that you can identify.

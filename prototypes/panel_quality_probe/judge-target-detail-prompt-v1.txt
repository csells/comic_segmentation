Check ONE proposed comic-panel crop against its source. You receive the complete
source page, the actual target crop, and an enlarged unannotated source detail
with context outside the crop's edges. The detail is extracted from the source,
not stitched from generated crops. Its label gives its original pixel bounds.
Candidate geometry is normalized to 0..1000 on the complete source page.

Identify the intended source scene using the target's reading-order number and
geometry. Trace that scene's actual drawn boundary through the source detail,
including curved or tapered edges. Compare all four edges of the actual crop.
Use the whole source page to establish scene ownership and narrative context.
Do not confuse source context with the proposed output: content visible only in
the source detail but absent from the actual crop can still be a real defect.

A defect requires specific target content present in the source but absent from
the actual crop. Check narrow background strips, frame-breaking artwork and all
associated lettering. A shared balloon belongs to each scene its tails or dots
point toward and must be complete in each associated crop. Content appearing in
another reader step does not excuse missing target content here.

Rectangles can include pieces of neighboring scenes. Trace the source-drawn
gutter or boundary to decide which scene owns a cut fragment; matching colors
alone do not establish ownership. An incomplete neighboring scene or a subject
already cut off by the artist is not a defect in this target. Do not demand that
an unrelated neighboring scene be included in full.

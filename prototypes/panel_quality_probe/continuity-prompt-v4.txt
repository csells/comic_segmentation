Assess the main artwork in this unannotated comic region. Ignore tiny fragments of neighboring panels cut off along the outer image edge. Is the main artwork ONE continuous composition, rather than multiple distinct panels or views?

Trace perspective, object scale and physical connections across any apparent division. Architecture, furniture, shadows and foreground objects within the same camera view do not divide it into panels. Conversely, an authored panel border or gutter may be diagonal, curved, black, white or colored. It need not form a rectangular grid. A drawn divider (frame, border or gutter) between differently framed artwork establishes separate panels even when their backgrounds and characters match. A view is an independently presented composition: a framed or bordered panel, an inset with its own boundary, a panel whose boundary is partly hidden by another panel, or a borderless composition separated from its neighbors by a clear gutter or spatial break. One continuous composition stays one view even when it shows several moments, repeated figures, a composited portrait or silhouette, or changes of scale. A difference in subject, scale, time or perspective alone does not create a separate view. Conversely, a distinct inset or adjacent region remains separate even when it repeats the same characters, colors or setting. Judge visual composition, not story continuity.
